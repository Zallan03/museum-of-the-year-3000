# PROMPT — Museum of the Year 3000

## Brief (from the kit's RULES.txt, judge brief in full)

Write a single line, at most 200 characters, in English: the museum label a
museum in the year 3000 puts next to an ordinary object from today. A judge
model compares labels pairwise and the funnier wins. The judge weighs, most
important first:

1. Is it the museum label a museum in the year 3000 puts beside an ordinary
   object from today.
2. Does the joke land on a first read, with the laugh in the line itself
   rather than in an explanation around it.
3. Does the humour come from the year 3000 misreading the object, so the
   label says something true and absurd about what the thing was for.
4. Does it read like a real museum label, short, straight faced and
   confident, in one line with no throat clearing.
5. Is the object and the angle fresh, rather than the obvious first joke
   about the obvious first object.

"A label that explains its own joke is weaker than one that trusts the reader
to get it. Length for its own sake does not count."

## Authoring prompt used in this session

Write six candidate labels in English, each one line of at most 200
characters, each in museum-label form: "Object, c. 20XX." followed by a
straight-faced note. The humour must come from the year 3000 misreading what
the object was for — the label says something true and absurd about its
purpose. Prefer less-obvious objects and angles (parking meter, traffic cone)
over the first joke about the first object. Never explain the joke. Rank them
against the five judge criteria above, pick the strongest, then revise it once
with a real change to the text (not cosmetics) before finalizing.
